Expedition Routes & Technical Topography

Eight distinct alpine objectives ranging from classic pyramid ascents to high-consequence knife-edge scrambles over iron chains, vertical ladders, and snow couloirs.

ROUTE 01 • THE CLASSIC SPEAR

Mt. Yarigatake Spearhead (3,180m)

槍ヶ岳 • 3 DAYS / 2 NIGHTS • CLASS 3 SCRAMBLE

Known as the Matterhorn of Japan, Yarigatake rises majestically at the intersection of Nagano and Gifu prefectures. Starting from the turquoise Azusa River in Kamikochi, hikers make an idyllic valley approach through lush cedar forests before ascending the terminal moraines of Yarisawa.

The final 100 vertical meters ascend a sheer granite pyramid equipped with fixed steel ladders and heavy iron chains. Guided climbers are belayed across exposed steps, summiting to an unobstructed 360-degree panorama of the entire Chubu Sangaku spine.

Total Distance
38 KM
Elevation Gain
+1,680 M
Technical Exposure
Moderate

ROUTE 02 • THE NOTORIOUS GAP

Daikiretto Knife-Edge Col (2,740m)

大キレット • 4 DAYS / 3 NIGHTS • CLASS 4 KNIFE-EDGE

The Daikiretto ("Great Cut") is the most famous and dangerous col in Japan. Dropping steeply between Mt. Minamidake and Mt. Kitahotaka, it forces climbers across narrow razorback granite ridges with vertical drops of over 800 meters into Takidani on one side and Kamikochi on the other.

Featuring the legendary Hasegawa Triangle and chimney crack ladders, light-pack agility is strictly required. Our dedicated porters port your heavy gear via valley routes, ensuring your hands and balance remain completely free for continuous 3-point contact.

42 KM
Elevation Gain
+2,240 M
Technical Exposure
High (Class 4)

ROUTE 03 • HIGHEST ALPS PEAK

Hotakadake Cirque Traverse (3,190m)

奥穂高岳 • 3 DAYS / 2 NIGHTS • CLASS 3+ SCRAMBLE

Mt. Oku-Hotakadake is Japan's third-highest peak and the crown of the Northern Alps. Encircling the dramatic amphitheater of Karasawa Cirque, this route navigates massive granite blocks, vertical chimney ascents, and the historic Hotakadake Sanso ridge hut.

ROUTE 04 • THE ULTIMATE TEST

Gendarme Pinnacle Dome (3,163m)

ジャンダルム • 3 DAYS / 2 NIGHTS • CLASS 4+ EXTREME

The mythical sentinel tower of the Hotaka ridge. Towering between Oku-Hotaka and Nishi-Hotakadake, the Gendarme requires nerves of steel over razorback granite cols, exposed traverse ledges, and vertical iron chain pitches directly over thousand-meter drops.

ROUTE 05 • MOUNTAIN OF SWORDS

Mt. Tsurugidake Crags (2,999m)

剱岳 • 3 DAYS / 2 NIGHTS • CLASS 4+ TECHNICAL

Regarded as Japan’s most technically demanding non-winter peak, Tsurugidake features 13 numbered iron chain sections anchored directly into sheer precipices. The route negotiates the famous Kani-no-Tatebai (vertical crab crawl ladder) and Kani-no-Yokobai (sideways crab traverse over air).

Seasonal Weather & Trail Conditions

Understanding the microclimates of the Northern Alps is crucial for trip planning. Our expeditions operate strictly between July 1 and October 15.

Season Window Summit Temp Range Trail Conditions Hut Availability Guide Recommendation
Early Summer (July 1 – July 20) 8°C to 18°C Lingering snow gullies in Karasawa; microspikes recommended on passes. Good Availability Yarigatake Valley Route (Alpine flowers in bloom).
High Summer (July 21 – Aug 31) 12°C to 22°C Completely dry rock on ridges. Afternoon thunderstorm potential (early 05:00 starts). Extremely High Demand Daikiretto, Gendarme & Hotaka Cirque (Prime dry rock grip).
Early Autumn (Sep 1 – Sep 20) 5°C to 15°C Crisp, stable continental high pressure air. Exceptional visibility across Japan Sea. Moderate Demand Tsurugidake & Full North Alps Traverses.
Autumn Foliage (Sep 21 – Oct 15) -2°C to 10°C World-famous red & gold foliage. Night frost on rungs; down jackets required. Huts Sold Out Early Karasawa Cirque to Yarigatake.
